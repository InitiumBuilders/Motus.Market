## Page 1 · What the legends actually are

**Apple — the product film you scroll.** Every flagship product page since AirPods Pro is a movie where *you* hold the playhead. One object, photoreal, suspended in void. Scroll advances the film frame by frame; a single sentence at a time floats beside the object, then dissolves before the next arrives. Nothing shares the stage — ever. The lesson that changed the industry: **scroll is a timeline, not a page.** And its typography law: one short line, enormous, alone — the caption *is* the interface.

**Igloo Inc. — Awwwards Site of the Year 2024.** A near-black WebGL cavern; one glowing object; the scroll descends *into* it. Zero feature grids. What made it legendary was not the 3D — it was the **confidence of emptiness**: whole viewports with a single word. The visitor becomes an explorer, not a reader.

**Zentry — the cinematic game-world portal.** Full-bleed video panels, massive serif-cut headlines that arrive one clause at a time, hard scene cuts between acts. Reads like a film trailer. Lesson: **structure the page as ACTS with cuts, not sections with headings** — the cut itself carries drama.

**Linear — restraint as engineering pride.** Blue-black void, hairlines, one lavender accent, type-first hero, motion so subtle it feels like the interface breathing. Lesson: **calm + precision reads as intelligence**; the flex is what you *don't* animate.

**Vercel / Stripe — the gradient as brand.** Stripe's animated mesh (2016) became the most copied visual on the web because it made a *payments API* feel alive. Vercel's triangle prism. Lesson: **one signature generative visual, owned forever** — a living thing no screenshot can capture, tied to the brand's meaning.

**Family.co — the wallet with a soul.** Tiny cast of 3D objects, enormous type, one idea per scroll-beat, warmth in a category that is usually cold chrome. Lesson: **emotional pacing beats feature density** even in crypto — *especially* in crypto.

**Bruno Simon's portfolio — the drivable world.** You drive a toy car through his résumé. Legendary because the *mechanic embodies the message* (playful creative developer). Lesson: **the interaction must BE the thesis**, not decorate it.

**teenage engineering — the catalog as manifesto.** Almost no motion at all: brutal grids, product photography, silence. Proof of the counter-law: **drama can come from stillness** when everything around you is loud.

**Reflect (our prior study).** One promise, one glowing body, one action — the calm-luxury benchmark. See notes-reflect.md.

## Page 2 · The nine laws the legends share

1. **One thing per viewport.** Every legend gives each idea a full screen of silence. The moment two headlines share a viewport, both lose. (Apple: one sentence at a time. Igloo: one word.)
2. **Scroll is the timeline.** The page is a film; scroll is the playhead; sections are ACTS separated by cuts of darkness. Progress feels like narrative, not navigation.
3. **The arrival is a ceremony.** The first 3 seconds are choreographed like a title sequence — a mark draws itself, a line resolves, then the world hands you control. Never dump the whole page at frame one.
4. **One signature visual, owned.** Stripe's mesh, Apple's floating product, Vercel's prism, Igloo's cavern. One living, generative, un-screenshotable thing that *is* the brand. Everything else stays quiet so it can reign.
5. **Type is the image.** When the words matter, the words are the visual — enormous, composed, alone in space. 2026's direction is explicit: heroes are layout systems where negative space does most of the talking.
6. **The mechanic embodies the message.** Whatever moves must *mean*. A market about minds in motion should literally set minds in motion on screen.
7. **Cut everything that competes.** The legends have no feature walls above the fold. Proof and detail live below the cinema, for the already-convinced.
8. **Control is returned quickly.** The ceremony runs seconds, not minutes; scrolling is never hijacked or locked; without JavaScript, everything is simply *there*. Awe must never cost usability.
9. **End where you began.** Ring composition — the closing viewport restates the opening line, and the door (CTA) stands beside it.

## Page 3 · What Motus.Market takes — the arrival design

**The signature visual — "Many Minds", literally.** A living canvas: dozens of small lights (minds) drifting in the indigo void, orbiting one eclipse — many minds, set in motion, around one center. Generative, never twice the same, un-screenshotable. The thesis *performs itself* (law 6) — this is our Stripe mesh.

**The mark.** A drawn emblem, not typed letters: the eclipse circle crossed by an orbital path, one bright mind-node riding it — the whole story in one glyph. It draws itself on arrival (law 3), then stands still forever.

**The acts.** Each mantra receives its own viewport of silence (law 1), arriving out of blur into focus — clarity as the motion itself:
Arrival (canvas + mark + "Many Minds Set In Motion") → "Your Heart, Mind, And Soul In Full Motion" → "Move The Mindset" → "Move The World" → "Build Your MotusMind" → "Make Your $Money Move" → the door. Then, below the cinema: the proof (rails, keystone, agents, fees, phases, library) for the already-convinced (law 7).

**The restraints we keep** (from Linear/Reflect/our own laws): full-strength ink always, one rainbow per surface, glow never highlight, no scroll-hijacking, everything visible without JavaScript, reduced-motion honored, animation paused off-screen. Calm remains the luxury signal — the cinema serves the words, never the reverse.

*The one-line distillation: **give every line a room of its own, make the thesis perform itself, and hand back control before awe becomes delay.***
